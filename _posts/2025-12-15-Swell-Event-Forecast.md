**Last updated 12/16 at 09:30**

Friday, 12/19, and Saturday morning are looking potentially very fun with a possibility of firing surf Friday.

## The Setup

![20251219 GFS](/assets/images/20251219_resized.jpg)

Courtesy of tropicaltidbits.com

We have a particularly large area of low pressure over the Great Lakes that will interact with an area of High pressure in the north-cental Atlantic late Thursday night that will create a decent fetch of south swell for our region. Recent runs are showing an area of gale force winds off the Southeast that will help push the swell numbers up if it comes to pass. All in all, we're looking at a short-mid period swell event.

Winds are expected to switch offshore as that low pressure system continues to move east. Expect strong, not far from gale, westerly winds that should set up hollow but not particularly user friendly waves.

## The Waves

![20251219 Swell Gif](/assets/images/2025-12-19_swell.gif)

Swell heights are expected to peak in the morning and should be well overhead early. There will be plenty of waves all day with chest-head high surf by sunset. I expect the numbers to move a little bit as we get closer, so don't get married to any one forecast just yet. Period values should be in the 8-9 second range and possibly 10 seconds or so by end of day. 

We have a mid-day low tide setting up two decent windows if everything cooperates, one in the AM and one in the PM.

![20251220 Swell Gif](/assets/images/2025-12-20_swell.gif)

Surf heights for Saturday are currently forecasted to be in the stomach-chest range or so. Again, it's a little early to make any calls and we're going to have strong offshore winds. At this point, I'd be surprised if we don't have some leftovers on Saturday, given the fetch we're looking at, but the size could be on the optimistic side right now. Period values should be in the 9-10 second range or so.

Wind is still fairly strong, which will limit the size of the leftover surf a bit. It helps that this is better directed and a little longer period at 10 seconds. But still, expect it to fade pretty quickly through the day.

Surf should be down enough for beginners and newer surfers by the afternoon. I'd avoid actual low tide, but there's a decent window for the longboards on the afternoon incoming tide.

## Sunday 12/21

![20251221 GFS](/assets/images/20251221_resized.jpg)

We're looking at another setup that's similar to what we've been seeing with a steep angled south-southwest angled swell from an area of low pressure over the Great Lakes. This area of low pressure is interacting with an area of high pressure east of the Maritimes which helps aim the swell a little bit better towards our region than some of the past swell events we've had.

That said, this will be a short-lived and short period swell. It should be pretty fun on a longboard or (potentially) a small wave board.

Current wind numbers are a little higher than I'd like to see.

## Looking Ahead

The end of the month looks active with another likely swell around 12/23. The swell on the 23rd looks particularly well directed and should be a proper SE swell, although not too powerful. After that, high pressure dominates and pushes low pressure systems north, so any swell is likely to be steep south or SW like we've been seeing. That pattern shifts around 12/30 where we have another potentially stronger setup.

Model runs are still showing a lot of variation this far out, but I'm confident that it will be an active time period. Whether that translates to waves or not is yet to be seen.
