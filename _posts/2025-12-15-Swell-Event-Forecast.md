**Last updated 12/17 at 13:00**

Friday, 12/19, and Saturday morning are looking potentially very fun with a possibility of firing surf Friday.

## The Setup

![20251219 GFS](/assets/images/20251219_resized.jpg)

Courtesy of tropicaltidbits.com

We have a particularly large area of low pressure over the Great Lakes that will interact with an area of High pressure in the north-cental Atlantic late Thursday night that will create a decent fetch of south swell for our region. Recent runs are showing an area of gale force winds off the Southeast that will help push the swell numbers up if it comes to pass. All in all, we're looking at a short-mid period swell event.

Winds are expected to switch offshore as that low pressure system continues to move east. Expect strong, not far from gale, westerly winds that should set up hollow but not particularly user friendly waves.

## The Waves

![20251219 End of Day](/assets/images/2025-12-19_21.png)

Swell heights are expected to peak in the morning and should be well overhead early. There will be plenty of waves all day with head high+ surf by sunset. I expect the numbers to move a little bit as we get closer, so don't get married to any one forecast just yet. Period values should be in the 8-9 second range in the AM and possibly 10 seconds or so by end of day. Swell angle is favorable for Monmouth County and should set up some fun right handers.

We have a mid-day low tide setting up a nice window in the late afternoon before sunset. Winds are expected to be strong out of the south early before switching offshore mid-day. Expect offshores to be around 30kts though. We haven't had blind takeoffs in a while, but they will be the norm on Friday.

Beginners and novice surfers should plan on watching from the beach. Expect strong currents and heavy, hollow waves.

While the largest surf will be in the AM, plenty of size sticks around as the day goes on and the surf will be much cleaner by mid-afternoon.

I'd throw a HPSB and/or a barrel board in the truck.

![20251220 AM](/assets/images/2025-12-20_14.png)

Surf heights for Saturday are currently forecasted to be in the waist-chest range or so. Again, it's a little early to make any calls and we're going to have strong offshore winds. At this point, I'd be surprised if we don't have some leftovers on Saturday, given the fetch we're looking at, but the size could be on the optimistic side right now. Period values should be in the 9-10 second range or so.

Wind is still fairly strong, which will limit the size of the leftover surf a bit. It helps that this is better directed and a little longer period at 10 seconds. But still, expect it to fade pretty quickly through the day. The best window will be the dropping tide in the AM. To make the most of it, look for a bar that can handle a bit higher tide.

Surf should be down enough for beginners and newer surfers by the afternoon. I'd avoid actual low tide, but there's a decent window for the longboards on the afternoon incoming tide.

## Sunday 12/21

![20251221 GFS](/assets/images/20251221_resized.jpg)

![20251221 Waves](/assets/images/2025-12-21_17.png)

We're looking at another setup that's similar to what we've been seeing with a steep angled south-southwest angled swell from an area of low pressure over the Great Lakes. This area of low pressure is interacting with an area of high pressure east of the Maritimes which helps aim the swell a little bit better towards our region than some of the past swell events we've had.

That said, this will be a short-lived and short period swell. It should be pretty fun on a longboard or (potentially) a small wave board. The best window is currently looking to be late AM/noon after the 7am high tide.

Current wind numbers are a little higher than I'd like to see, but forecasts are still bouncing around some. Winds are currently expected to remain offshore all day, but again, that could change.
