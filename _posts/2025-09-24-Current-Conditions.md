Let’s talk about why I don’t include a “current conditions” section in my surf forecasts.

## The Problem with Generalizing Surf Spots

If you’ve surfed for more than a season, you already know: **your local spot changes**. Not just day to day, but year to year. I used to surf one particular jetty for two or three years straight. It was consistent, worked on a bunch of swell angles, and if there was energy in the water, you could count on something breaking.

Fast forward to now, and I’ve surfed that same jetty maybe twice in the last year and a half. It’s consistently a foot or two smaller than a spot just a mile up the beach. And it’s not because the swell is different. The beach doesn’t suddenly rotate 30 degrees. There’s no dramatic shift in refraction or storm fetch. What’s changed is **the bathymetry** (the sandbars, the bottom contours, the way the swell interacts with the shoreline).

## Sandbars, Jetties, and the East Coast Shuffle

Especially on the East Coast, where beach replenishment is common and sand moves around like it’s got somewhere to be, you can’t rely on one spot to behave the same way for long. A jetty that used to be magic might now be mush. Meanwhile, a random stretch of beach might suddenly start firing.

That’s why I don’t do “here’s what it looks like right now” posts. Because unless I’m standing at your exact spot, looking at the lineup, I’m probably wrong. And I’m guessing you don’t want me to tell you what your spot looks like anyway.

## Forecasts vs. Reality

I could say “waist to head high” for a given day, and that forecast would be technically correct. But depending on where you paddle out, it might feel like a lie. That’s why I prefer to give an idea of what a **decent, but average bar** would do. The best bar on a given day? It’ll be better—maybe a little, maybe a lot.

So if you’re surfing sand-bottom beach breaks, especially in areas with shifting sand and replenishment projects (all of us), you’ve got to look around.

## Cameras: Use Them

On the East Coast, cams are king when it comes to current conditions. Surfline’s bought up most of them, and other sites have aggregated the rest. I link to [TheSurfersView](https://thesurfersview.com) because they’ve got a solid spread and don’t charge for access.

Personally, I’m not into running my own cameras. I saw a gap in the surf forecasting world for accurate, text-based forecasts, and that’s where I wanted to contribute. For real-time conditions, there are already solid options out there. Use them for your day-of decisions.

## You Can’t Outsurf the Wave

Here’s where the rubber meets the wax: you can’t outsurf the wave. You can optimize your line, squeeze every ounce of speed and flow out of a section, but if the wave’s not offering much, you’re not getting much.

That’s another way advanced surfers separate themselves from beginners and intermediates. It's not just in how they surf, but in where they surf. They know that finding the right wave on the right day is half the battle. And they’re willing to put in the time to chase it.

So no, I don’t do current conditions because I don't think it even *can* be reliable. The forecasts I do are meant to give you the information you need to look for a wave, as well as what you need to avoid.
