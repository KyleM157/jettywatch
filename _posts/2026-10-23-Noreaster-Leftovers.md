## First, A Look at the Charts

![20261023_waves](/assets/images/20261023_1200_waves_resized.jpg "Wave chart showing swell have to wrap around Cape Cod")

source: windy.com

The above chart is the combined swell and wind wave chart from the 06z GFS model run. There are two pressure systems visible, one strong region of high pressure north of New York and a strong low east of Nova Scotia. As these interact, a steep pressure gradient forms and along that gradient we get increased winds and, eventually, waves.

The long yellow arrow shows the primary fetch from this system which should translate to a swell direction of around 10 degrees give or take. The winds involved are fairly strong and the waves in that region are forecasted to be 12ft at 10 second period. Immediately west of that arrow, we can see a bit more northeast to the pressure gradient which should help send another round of surf to North Carolina and eventually North Florida by mid-day Saturday.

Looking at the small yellow arrows to the west of the primary fetch, that is demonstrating the refraction due to shallower depths along the coast. Swell can wrap in, especially when it's longer period. The main question as we look at the rest of our Friday and the poor conditions that are expected: what happens over the weekend.

![20261024 wave power](/assets/images/20261023_1000_resized.jpg "wave power chart showing the steep gradient of wave heights due to refracting swell")

source: windy.com

I switched to the wave power chart because it shows the gradient a bit better. Wave power is just a combination of wave height and period. This is for Saturday 10/24 at 10am.

## Cape Cod and Shadowing Effects

This is a beautiful example of how Cape Cod is just the *worst*. We actually get this setup pretty commonly. These types of NE swells really just end up going to NC and FL rather than to where it's really needed, where I surf. It's also pretty interesting to see how little VA sees from this swell compared to NC but I digress.

For reference, that gradient means near flat for NY to 7ft for Hatteras Island.

## Hudson Canyon/Valley?

The Hudson Canyon can help swell refract into both North NJ and NY, particularly Rockaway Beach. The primary effect is to help east swell get into NY and south swell refract into north NJ. In this case, I think there is too much north in the swell to really see an effect from the canyon in either of these regions.

## Conclusion

Overall, model forecasts have stayed pretty optimistic for the weekend. This is likely partially due to the resolution of the models and we're looking at a pretty narrow region where a really small change is data could mean a significant difference in wave heights. Of the models I look at, at each step up in resolution (step down in size of grid), wave heights decrease (with the exception of Nearshore Wave).
