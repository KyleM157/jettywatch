# Why I Use AI to Write About Surfing (And Why That’s Not a Problem)

Let’s talk about the elephant in the lineup: AI-generated content.

It’s controversial, sure. Especially when it’s used to crank out generic articles with no soul. But that’s not what I’m doing here. This blog isn’t written *by* AI—it’s written *with* AI. And there’s a big difference.

## My Background: Math, Code, and Surfboards

I’ve never been a natural writer. My strengths have always been in math, science, and programming. I’ve spent over a decade working in engineering-heavy fields, and I’ve built up a pretty deep understanding of surfboard design, wave dynamics, and forecasting along the way.

The problem? Writing about it clearly and accessibly has always been a struggle.

Surfing, and especially board design, is full of pseudoscience and vague terminology. Words get tossed around without much precision, and it’s rare to see real technical depth outside of niche forums. I wanted to change that. But grammar and flow kept slowing me down.

## My Workflow: Voice-to-Text + Copilot

Here’s how I make these posts:

1. I use voice-to-text to talk through my ideas.
2. I edit the transcript for clarity.
3. I run it through Copilot to clean up grammar and structure.
4. I review the final version to make sure the **facts stay intact**.

The goal is simple: share original insights in a format that’s actually readable.

## The Jargon Problem

When you go deep into any technical topic, jargon creeps in. I tend to think and speak in a way that’s pretty analytical, which can be off-putting if you’re not wired the same way.

Take this example:

> “The velocity of a displacement hull at paddle speeds varies with the square root of its length—specifically, the rail line.”

That’s technically accurate. But when I wrote it out, people told me it made them feel dumb. That wasn’t the goal. I just wanted to explain why longer boards paddle faster.

Using AI to reformat and simplify that kind of content has helped me reach more people without dumbing things down. The facts stay the same—the delivery just gets smoother.

## Is This Cheating?

Some folks are skeptical of AI-assisted writing. I get it. If the facts were generated by AI, I’d be skeptical too.

But here’s the thing:

- **All the ideas are mine**
- **All the facts are mine**
- **The AI just helps me say it better**

It’s no different than using a calculator or a Python script to solve an integral. You still need to understand the problem, set up the right inputs, and verify the output. The tool just saves you from spending hours on handwritten math—and reduces the chance of errors.

## Final Thoughts

I’m not trying to replace human insight with machine output. I’m trying to **amplify** my own voice—especially in a space where technical clarity is rare.

If you’re here for surfboard performance, forecasting nuance, or just want to understand how to get your new quad set up without breaking the bank, I promise: the ideas are real. The writing is just cleaner than it used to be.
